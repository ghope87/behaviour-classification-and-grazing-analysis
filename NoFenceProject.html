Using NoFence Collar Data to Investigate Accelerometer Activity, and Reserve Site Habitat Selection and Use in Highland Cattle

Author

Graham Hope

Executive Summary

This report evaluates NoFence GPS and accelerometer collar data collected between June 2021 and May 2024 to investigate seasonal cattle behaviour and spatial grazing patterns. Seasonal activity levels were compared to identify differences in grazing intensity throughout the year. A Hidden Markov Model was applied to classify active and resting behavioural states from collar sensor data. Behavioural states were mapped to examine seasonal and annual patterns of site use. The analyses identified significant seasonal variation in activity, and stable long-term spatial grazing patterns , providing evidence to inform grazing management and further monitoring.

Background

Conservation grazing requires an understanding of how cattle behaviour, habitat use and grazing pressure vary through time to support evidence-based management decisions. Although NoFence collars provide continuous GPS location and accelerometer data, these datasets are often used primarily for livestock tracking rather than ecological analysis. By combining collar data with behavioural observations and vegetation monitoring, grazing patterns can be quantified and linked to habitat condition.

This project uses NoFence collar data to investigate seasonal variation cattle activity, classify behavioural states from accelerometer data, map spatial patterns of between cattle presence and habitat use. Together, these analyses demonstrate how geospatial and statistical methods can support adaptive conservation grazing management and long-term ecological monioring.

Project Objectives

  • Quantify seasonal variation in cattle activity.

  • Classify active and resting behavioural states using Hidden Markov Models.

  • Map seasonal and annual spatial distribution of behavioural states.

  • Assess habitat use across the reserve.

Methods and Results

Software

All analyses were conducted using Python 3.12 and R 4.5.2. Data manipulation was performed uusing pandas and NumPy; spatial analyses used GeoPandas; statistical analyses were conducted using SciPy and statsmodels; behavioural modelling used momentuHMM in R; and interactive maps were produced using Folium. The report was produced using Quarto.

Workflow

Data Cleaning and Preprocessing

NoFence collar data derived from 14 highland cattle monitored between July 2021 to February 2024 were imported into Python. Invalid timestamps were removed, primary analysis variables were retained and observations were assigned to seasonal subsets based on calender month: Winter (December - February); Spring (March - May); Summer (June - August); and Autumn (September - November).

Seasonal Activity Time-series and Analysis

The seasonal subsets were converted to accelerometer activity time-series, which were averaged across all cattle and aggregated to produce an activity profile.

def MakeTimeSeries(data, variable):
    df_series=pd.DataFrame({'Cow': data['Cow'],
                'Time': data['Time'],
                variable: data[variable]
                })

    # Group series into activity recorded to the nearest half hour of the day
    df_series['time30']=pd.to_datetime(df_series['Time']).dt.round('30min')
    
    df_series['time30']=df_series['time30'].dt.time
    df_series=(df_series
           .groupby(['time30','Cow'])[variable]
           .median()
           .reset_index()
          )
    df_series['hour']=df_series['time30'].apply(lambda t: t.hour +t.minute/60)
    
    cows=df_series['Cow'].unique()

    
    median_series=(df_series
             .groupby('hour')[variable]
             .agg(median='median',
                 q1=lambda x: x.quantile(0.25),
                  q3=lambda x: x.quantile(0.75)
                 )
             .reset_index()
            )

    return median_series

Behavioural Monitoring Survey

A survey123 form was designed to collect behavioural labels that correspond with timestamped accelerometer data. Since signals delivering data from the collars were variable, forms were completed every 15 minutes to minimize collar alignment errors. The time, behaviour and time spent feeding were recorded over two separate 5 day periods in March and May from 7 animals across 3 reserves.

Temporal Alignment of Behavioural Labels with Accelerometer Data

Survey123 behaviour observations were aggregated into two overlapping 30-minute time blocks (quarter-past to quarter-to the hour, and half-past to the hour) to maximise temporal alignment with the NoFence collar observations.

def TimeBlocks(data,cowList):
  
  allCowData=[]
  for cow in cowList:
    cowData=data[data['Cow']==cow]
    cowData['Date and time']=pd.to_datetime(cowData['Date and time'], format='mixed')
    cowData = cowData.sort_values('Date and time')
    
    blocka=cowData.copy()
    blocka['block30']=(
      blocka['Date and time']
      .sub(pd.Timedelta(seconds=1))
      .dt.floor('30min')
      .add(pd.Timedelta(minutes=30)))
    blocka = (
      blocka
      .groupby(['Cow','block30'], as_index=False)
      .agg(
        Behaviour30=('Behaviour', lambda x:','.join(x.dropna().unique())),
        grazing_time30=('Grazing time (minutes)', 'sum')))
    
    
    blockb=cowData.copy()
    blockb['block15']=(
      blockb['Date and time']
      .sub(pd.Timedelta(minutes=15))
      .dt.ceil('30min')
      .add(pd.Timedelta(minutes=15)))
    blockb = (
      blockb
      .groupby(['Cow','block15'], as_index=False)
      .agg(
        Behaviour15=('Behaviour', lambda x:','.join(x.dropna().unique())),
        grazing_time15=('Grazing time (minutes)', 'sum')))
    
    blocka['row_no']=blocka.groupby('Cow').cumcount()
    blockb['row_no']=blockb.groupby('Cow').cumcount()
    
    cowDataBlocks=blocka.merge(blockb,on=['Cow','row_no'], how='inner')
    allCowData.append(cowDataBlocks)
    
  return pd.concat(allCowData, ignore_index=True)

The method used to merge the datasets was validated using root mean squared error (RMSE), mean absolute difference (MAD), and maximum error. These metrics were also expressed as a percentage of the 30-minute sampling interval. Low RMSE and MAD values relative to the 30-minute sampling interval indicated good temporal alignment between the behavioural observations and collar data. The results are displayed in Table 2.

Behavioural Classification and Model Validation

The data were prepared for hidden Markov model (HMM) analysis following the workflow described in the momentuHMM documentation version 1.5.8 (McClintock & Michelot, 2018). Variables were renamed to the required format, individual cattle identifiers were converted to factors, observations were ordered chronologically, and the data were converted to the format required by prepData(). Accelerometer activity values were log-transformed (log1p) to improve normality before model fitting.

# Prepare data for compiling into prepData
dat<-data %>%

  arrange(ID, time)

Following model fitting, the estimated model parameters were extracted and applied to an independent test data set. The test dataset, previously temporally aligned with the behavioural labels, was prepared for the hidden Markov model prediction following the momentuHMM documentation, version 1.5.8 (McClintock & Michelot, 2018). Behavioural states were predicted using the parameters estimated for the trained hidden Markov model. Predicted states were then compared with observed behavioural labels using a confusion matrix (Table 3).

A hidden Markov model was applied separately to each season subset. Predicted behavioural states were subsequently mapped for each season and for the complete 2021 - 2024 study period.

Spatial Analysis and Mapping

Cumulative use was defined as the total number of hours cattle spent in a given behavioural state within each grid square. Cumulative use was calculated and mapped separately for active and resting states, both seasonally and across the full 2021-2024 study period. Figure 2 shows cumulative active-state use at a Gloucestershire Wildlife Trust reserve across the complete study period.

To protect site confidentiality, raw GPS locations and individual movement tracks are not presented. Spatial patterns are summarised at an aggregated grid-cell and habitat-level.

def MakeMap(map_grid, behaviour):

    centre_x = map_grid.geometry.centroid.x.mean()
    centre_y = map_grid.geometry.centroid.y.mean()
    
    # Convert to WGS84 and calculate map centre
    map_grid=map_grid.to_crs('EPSG:4326')

Habitat Use and Selection

To assess habitat preference cumulative cattle use was summarised within the mapped habitat boundaries at the study site. Figure 3 shows the spatial distribution of cumulative use across the main habitat types, while Table 3 presents the proportion of total use occurring within each habitat for the active and resting behavioural states.

# Prepare data for mapping and analysis
def SummariseHabUse(data, site_habitats):
    # Recode states as rest = 0 and active = 1
    data['recode_states']=data['states'].apply(lambda x: 1 if x==2 else 0)
   
    # convert to geodataframe with crs same as habitat layer
    data_gdf=gpd.GeoDataFrame(data, geometry=gpd.points_from_xy(data['x'], data['y']) ,crs=site_habitats.crs)

    # merge with habitat layer and count number of hours spent in each habitat
    points_in_hab=gpd.sjoin(
    data_gdf,
    site_habitats,
    how='left',
    predicate='within')
    
    # Summarise by usage and accelerometer activity
    habitat_summary=(points_in_hab
                 .groupby('UKHAB1')
                 .agg(
                     all_count=('recode_states', 'size'),
                     active_count=('recode_states','sum'),
                     mean_activity=('activity', 'mean'))
                 .reset_index())
                 
    
    # Rest Usage
    habitat_summary['rest_count']=habitat_summary['all_count']-habitat_summary['active_count']
    
    # Sum area by habitat type
    site_habitats['area_m2']=site_habitats.geometry.area

    total_area=(site_habitats
            .groupby('Habitat')
            .agg(
                area=('area_m2','sum'))
            .reset_index())

    habitat_summary=habitat_summary.merge(total_area, on='UKHAB1', how='inner')

    # Calculate habitat use proportions
    habitat_summary['use_prop']=(habitat_summary['all_count']/np.sum(habitat_summary['all_count']))*100
    habitat_summary['area_prop']=habitat_summary['area']/np.sum(habitat_summary['area'])
    habitat_summary['selection_ratio']=habitat_summary['use_prop']/habitat_summary['area_prop']

    # Make data to be mapped
    map_data=site_habitats[['UKHAB1', 'geometry']].merge(habitat_summary, on='UKHAB1', how='right')

    return map_data

Descriptive patterns of habitat use were broadly similar between active and resting behavioural states. Heathland showed the greatest cumulative active-state, while grassland and woodland accounted for similarly high proportions of rest-state use.

Habitat use did not correspond directly with habitat availability. Woodland comprised the largest area occupy 37% of the mapped habitat, but accounted for only 21.0% of active-state use and 25.7% of rest-state use. In contrast, heathland and grassland received a greater proportion of cattle use than their proportional area suggesting preferential use of these habitats.

Interpretation and Key findings

Seasonal Variation in Activity

The observed seasonal variation in activity profiles is likely associated with variation in day length and temperature. Longer daylight hours may contribute to extended periods of daytime activity, whereas higher temperatures may encourage longer midday resting periods.

Substantially higher spring activity relative to other seasons may be related to seasonal variation in the palatability of vegetation. Considering that spring often coincides with periods of habitat and species sensitivity, it may represent a priority period for minimizing grazing impacts.

Hidden Markov Model Performance

The results of the two-state Hidden Markov Model indicate that the model provides a reliable basis for classifying behavioural states across the wider collar dataset.

The validated HMM therefore enabled behavioural state predictions to be extended across periods where direct field observations were unavailable, allowing seasonal and long-term patterns of cattle activity to be summarised spatially.

Overall 2021 - 2024 Habitat Preference

Habitat use was spatially uneven and did not reflect habitat availability. Heathland and grassland received disproportionately high active-state use, whilst woodland received lower active-state use despite comprising the largest proportion of available habitat. In contrast, grassland received the highest rest-state use compared to the proportion of habitat available. These findings are indicative of behaviour dependent habitat selection and could help to identify areas at risk of overgrazing, as well as underused habitats.

Limitations

On site surveys and discussions with land managers identified several potential limitations, particularly associated with collar functionality and usage.

  • Collars can malfunction erroneously recording zero activity even when an animal is active.

  • Collars can be swapped between animals, which can reduce predicted behavioural state accuracy.

  • A different set of collars were gradually introduced during late 2023 early 2024, which reduced the amount of data available. This is reflected in the fewer active hours observed in the map for the corresponding period June 2023 to May 2024.

  • The introduction of new collars also resulted in different activity recording characteristics. Consequently, classification performance estimated from recent validation data may not be directly transferable to earlier datasets.
